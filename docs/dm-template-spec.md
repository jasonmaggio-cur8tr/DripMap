# DripMap Outreach Voice Spec

Two types of outreach happen when a new shop is published on DripMap: an **email** sent via Loops.so, and an **Instagram DM** sent manually by the admin. Both follow the DripMap voice but serve different purposes.

---

## Outreach Email (via Loops)

The email is a notification — friendly, clear, and low-pressure. It tells the shop they're listed and gives them a path to claim ownership.

**Tone:** Warm, direct, no corporate speak.

**Template direction:**

> Hey [Shop Name],
>
> Just wanted to let you know that your shop is now listed on DripMap. You can claim ownership of the shop for free and customize the shop detail page to your preference.
>
> [Claim Your Shop]
>
> If you have any questions at all just email jason@dripmap.space

**Key rules:**
- No exclamation stacking
- No "we are pleased to inform you"
- One clear CTA (claim link)
- Human sign-off (jason@dripmap.space, not "the DripMap team")

The actual email template is built in Loops.so. The codebase sends these contact properties:
- `shopName`
- `shopDripmapUrl`
- `shopClaimUrl`
- `neighborhood`
- `city`

---

## Instagram DM (agent-generated, admin sends manually)

The DM is personal — it references something specific about the shop that only someone who looked would know. The agent generates a draft; the admin reviews and sends it from the DripMap Instagram account.

### Voice Rules

1. **Knowledgeable, not snobby** — reference something specific you'd only know if you'd looked at the shop (a signature drink, an aesthetic detail, the barista's name from their posts).
2. **Energetic, not frantic** — short sentences. No excitement-stacking. No exclamation overload.
3. **Community-first** — center the shop and the people in it, not DripMap.
4. **Direct** — no fluff, no corporate-speak, no "we are pleased to inform you."
5. **Light humor, zero cringe** — wit OK, puns and emoji-stacks not OK.

### Required Elements

Every DM template must include:

1. A **specific reference** to the shop (something concrete the agent observed — a drink, a barista name, an aesthetic detail, a post they made).
2. The **DripMap URL** for their listing.
3. One **clear next step** ("tap the link, claim your shop, takes 60 seconds").
4. **Sign-off as DripMap.**

### Constraints

- 3-5 short sentences
- Under 80 words
- No emoji-stacks (single emoji OK if it adds meaning)
- No puns

### Example

> Saw your pour-over flight — the Ethiopia natural is calling our name. We just listed Blueprint Coffee on DripMap so Wicker Park coffee folks can find you: dripmap.com/blueprint-coffee. Tap through and claim the shop (60 seconds, free) — you'll get edit access, photo control, and the analytics dashboard. Welcome to the map. — DripMap

### How the Agent Should Personalize

The agent should scan the shop's Instagram feed and website for:
- A signature drink or menu item
- An aesthetic detail (decor, plants, lighting, a mural)
- A recent post or story worth referencing
- The neighborhood's identity (what it's known for)

Pick the single most interesting thing and lead with it. Don't try to mention everything — one sharp detail beats three generic ones.
