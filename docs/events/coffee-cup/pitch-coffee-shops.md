# Pitch: Coffee Shops

Three formats — email, in-person, DM — plus the FAQ and the objection handling.

**The one thing that actually works: walk in, buy a coffee, hand the owner a printed one-pager, and ask for ninety seconds.** Email is the follow-up, not the pitch. Sacramento's coffee scene is a face-to-face scene. Budget an afternoon for the top eight shops and you'll close six of them.

---

## The 30-second version (in person)

> "I'm putting on The Coffee Cup — twelve Sacramento coffee & tea shops, 3v3 street soccer, one Sunday afternoon at Futsi, November 15th. There's a trophy. The shop that wins keeps it on their bar for a year until somebody takes it from them.
>
> It's a hundred bucks, that includes three shirts with every shop's name on the back. Your baristas play, your owners play, and if you need a couple more you can pull from your regulars. Three games guaranteed. Public can come watch for free.
>
> [Shop X] is already in. Want me to hold you a spot?"

Then stop talking. The trophy line and the rival shop line do the work.

## FAQ

**"None of us can actually play soccer."**
Perfect, neither can most of the field. It's 3v3 on a small court — it's more like pickup basketball than a real match. Games are twelve minutes. The teams that take it too seriously are going to have a worse day than you are.

**"We're open Sunday, our staff is working."**
That's why it starts at 2pm, after most shops close. Send whoever's off, pull a couple of your regulars, and put the rest of the crew in the crowd — the Best Supporters award is real and it's fan-voted.

**"Can we bring people who don't work here?"**
Yes. Two people on your roster need to work at or own the shop; the rest can come from your community, your regulars, your roaster, your friends. Plenty of teams will be half staff, half regulars.

**"Where do we find women players?"**
Tell me and I'll help. We run a player pool of people who want to play but aren't attached to a shop, and I'll match you. Nobody misses out on this because of roster math.

**"Is it $100 per person?"**
Per shop. One hundred dollars total, however many people you roster. Includes three shirts.

**"What if we get hurt?"**
Every player signs a waiver at registration, the venue is a proper insured futsal facility, no slide tackling is allowed, and there's a first aid kit and ice on site. It's a rec tournament, we run it like one.

**"Can we sponsor / do more than play?"**
Yes, and let's talk. There's a title sponsor slot and two supporting slots, and there's a coffee service angle if you'd rather pour than play.

**"Can we bring a cart / sell coffee there?"**
Talk to me — Futsi's bar has the F&B, but there's a version of this where a shop does a pour-over bar in the corner. Ask early.

## Objection handling

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We'll think about it." | "Totally. There are twelve spots and I'm holding one for you until Friday — can I text you Thursday?" *(Create a real deadline. Use it.)* |
| "$100 is a lot right now." | "It's less than a Sunday's worth of ads and your staff will remember it for a year. And if the money's genuinely the blocker, tell me — I'd rather have you there." |
| "We're not competitive people." | "Neither is anyone. The Decaf Award goes to whoever finishes last and it's the most fun award of the day." |
| "Nobody here plays." | "[Shop X] said the same thing and then found out their morning baristas both played in college. Ask your crew — you'll be surprised what's in the building." |
| "Is this a DripMap marketing thing?" | "Yeah, partly, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise — DripMap's putting it on and every shop that plays gets a profile on there. But the trophy's real and the event stands up on its own." |

Answer that last one straight. Coffee shop owners are allergic to being marketed at by someone pretending they aren't. Owning it is what makes them trust you.

## Target list — pitch 18–20 to land 12

Build the list in these buckets. Sacramento's scene is small and everyone knows everyone, so the first three yeses make the next nine much easier — go get your strongest relationships first and name them in every subsequent pitch.

| Bucket | Why | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor roasters** | Name recognition. Their yes unlocks the rest. | 3–4 |
| **Multi-location shops** | Deep staff bench, easy to field a team. | 2–3 |
| **Neighborhood indies** | Midtown, Oak Park, East Sac, Land Park — the heart of the field. | 6–8 |
| **New openings (last 18 months)** | Hungriest for exposure, fastest yes. | 2–3 |
| **A wildcard** | Mobile cart, a bakery-café, a shop from Davis or Folsom or Grass Valley. Outsider energy. | 1–2 |

**Sequencing that works:**
1. Lock your two closest relationships first. Get an explicit "yes, we're in" you can quote.
2. Go in person to the next six, name-dropping the first two.
3. Email and DM the rest once you're at six committed — "six spots left" beats "want to join?"
4. Close the field at twelve and keep two shops warm on a standby list through Oct 26.

Do not pitch anyone until Futsi confirms the date.
